You are writing a shot list for a 3D animated nursery rhyme video for
young children. I will paste your answer straight into a tool, so the
format matters more than the prose.

THE SONG
  Title: Animal Sound Parade
  Length: 125 seconds
  It is an animal-sounds song - a dog, a cat and a duck, and the noises
  they make.

  PASTE THE WHOLE OF lyrics.txt HERE, one line per line, before you
  send this. Everything below depends on it.

THE CHARACTER (already handled - do not describe him)
  Nik, a 3 year old Indian toddler boy. A separate part of the tool
  already puts his full description and the art style in front of every
  line you write. If you describe him, or mention colours, lighting or
  the art style, it will fight that description and the character will
  change between shots.

WHAT TO WRITE
  ONE LINE FOR EVERY LINE OF THE LYRICS ABOVE. If the song has 44
  lines, write 44. Not 16, not "about that many" - exactly as many, in
  exactly the same order.

  This is the most important rule here. Your line 12 is what is on
  screen while lyric line 12 is being sung, and the tool lines them up
  one to one. Get the count wrong and it cannot: the last video played
  "Meow, meow, meow!" over a duckling and "Quack, quack, quack!" over a
  puppy, because there were sixteen scenes for forty-four lines and the
  tool had to share them out by the clock.

  EACH LINE MUST SHOW WHAT ITS LYRIC SAYS. If the lyric is "Meow, meow,
  meow!" the kitten is the subject of that shot. If it is "Wag your
  tail," the puppy is, and its tail is wagging. If it is "Clap your
  hands," Nik is clapping. Read the lyric, then write the picture of
  it.

  One shot per line. Nothing else.

  Each line must contain, in this order:
    1. ONE physical action Nik does. One. Not two joined with "and then".
       It must be something a camera could film - "he claps his hands",
       not "he feels happy".

       It has to happen WHERE HE ALREADY IS. Never write that he walks
       in, walks over, runs to, comes towards, steps up to or walks
       away. A model told to walk someone into the shot walks them out
       of it again, and the second half of the clip is an empty field -
       that is exactly what happened to the first one of these.

       Make it big, and make it SLOW. Each shot is four seconds long
       and the action has to fill them without hurrying. "He claps his
       hands twice" and "he waves one hand slowly above his head" are
       right. "He claps five times" and anything with "quickly" or
       "rapidly" in it comes back frantic - the first one did.

       Never more than two of anything. Never the words "quickly",
       "rapidly" or "fast".
    2. Who else is in the shot and what they are doing, if anyone.
       Name them: the puppy, the kitten, the duckling.
    3. One small piece of background life - flowers nodding, butterflies
       drifting, leaves swaying, clouds moving, grass rippling.
    4. The words "the camera does not move" - unless that shot genuinely
       needs a slow push in, in which case write "the camera pushes in
       slowly".

  Start about a third of the lines with "Close up of" or "Wide shot of"
  so the video is not sixteen identical framings - but "Close up of"
  is for the ANIMALS only. A close up of Nik comes back with the top of
  his head cut off. For him, use "Wide shot of" or nothing at all.

  Write in the present tense. Begin the rest with "He".

  Keep each line between 18 and 28 words.

RULES
  - Plain text only. No numbering, no bullets, no quotes, no bold, no
    headings, no blank lines between them, no commentary before or
    after. Just the 16 lines.
  - No text, words, letters or lyrics appearing in the picture.
  - Nobody enters or leaves a shot. The animals are already beside him
    at the start of the line and still there at the end. They may bark,
    hop on the spot, flap, sit, wag - not run to him or waddle over.
  - EVERY animal in the line must be doing something. The first video
    moved the boy and left the puppy sitting like an ornament, and the
    reason was that the line only said the puppy was there. "the puppy
    beside him" is not enough; "the puppy beside him wagging its tail"
    is.
  - Nothing violent, nothing frightening, nothing sad.
  - The shots should follow one another as a little story: he greets
    each animal in turn, they play, they end together. Not "he
    arrives" - see the rule about walking in.
  - Give each of the three animals at least one close up of its own.
